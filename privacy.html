Privacy Policy

Last updated: 26 September 2026

1. Who we are

RESQ+ is published by Sornsawan Ltd., a company registered in Thailand. It is the data controller for the personal data described in this policy. Contact: sales@sornsawan.com.

RESQ+ helps in an emergency; it does not replace the emergency services. In a life-threatening situation, always call your local emergency number.

For each feature, this policy says what we collect, why, where it goes and how long we keep it. It is written to match what the app actually does.

2. Your emergency profile

Your profiles (yours and those of relatives you add) can contain health data: blood type, allergies, conditions, medicines, surgeries, notes, family doctor, preferred hospital, emergency contacts and insurance. They are stored encrypted on your phone (AES-256, Android Keystore), and a copy is saved in your RESQ+ account so that it survives a lost or replaced phone. Only your signed-in account can read that copy. You can change or delete a profile at any time in the app.

3. What each feature uses, and why

Data is used only for the feature you are using:

  • Account and sign-in: your phone number (verified by SMS) or your Google account, an account identifier and a notification token. Used to sign you in, to protect the community from abuse and to deliver alerts to you.
  • SOS: when you trigger an SOS, your name and position are sent to the emergency contacts you chose: by SMS sent from your own phone through your carrier, by e-mail (through Resend) and through a private live-tracking link. The members of your circles also receive your blood type and critical allergies, unless you turn this off in Settings. Video recorded during the SOS is stored so that you and the contacts you authorised can see it. When you declare a trip, the contacts you choose are told when you leave, arrive or cancel, by e-mail and, if you turn it on, by SMS from your phone.
  • Circles (Team Connect): your membership, the alerts and messages you send, and your position when you share it (live position, trips, meeting points), visible to the members of that circle.
  • Community alerts: the alerts you post (type, text, position, and any photo, voice note or video) and your messages in their chats are visible to people near the alert. If you post anonymously, other people do not see your name; our systems keep your account identifier to prevent abuse. Messages in alert chats are translated automatically by Google's AI. Once a week, a statistical summary of recent alerts is prepared with Google's AI (alert types and counts, and the beginning of up to 10 alert texts with their address, are sent to it); the summary keeps only figures and the AI's comment.
  • Reports and blocking: when you report an alert, a message or a person, we record your account identifier, the reason and the date; the person reported is never told who reported them. Content reported by three different people is hidden automatically (an emergency is sent to human review instead); our team reviews reports and can ban an account. Blocking someone happens on your phone only: nothing is sent to us or to that person.
  • First Aid Assistant: what you type or say, the photos you add, the recent messages of the conversation, your country's emergency numbers and the items of your first-aid kit are sent to Google's AI (Gemini, on Google Cloud) to answer you. The medical record of the chosen profile is sent only if you agree (section 4, which also explains how versions before 4.6 differ). Conversations are stored encrypted on your phone only, are not included in phone backups and keep their last 100 messages. Our servers keep no copy of the questions, answers or photos, only a daily count of your messages to apply the daily limit.
  • Subscriptions: purchases go through Google Play; RevenueCat manages your subscription status with your account identifier. GoMarketMe, our affiliate program, can see Google Play sales records to credit partners; it receives nothing from inside the app. When you delete your account, we ask RevenueCat to delete its record too.
  • Permissions: location, camera, microphone, SMS and notifications are requested only for the features above; you can withdraw them at any time in Android settings.
  • Statistics and crash reports: Firebase Analytics and Crashlytics collect how the app is used and technical reports when it fails (device model, Android version, app events), to keep the app working.
  • Maps, routes and weather: maps, trip routes, meeting points and the choice of your region's emergency numbers use your position with Google Maps Platform; the weather tool sends your position to Open-Meteo at the moment you use it. The emergency directory uses your country.

4. Health data and the First Aid Assistant: your explicit consent

Health data are sensitive data. The assistant sends the medical record of the chosen profile only after you tap Share in the app, and you can withdraw that consent at any time from the assistant's menu ("Share the medical record"); it applies from the next question. Without consent, the assistant still answers, without taking the profile into account.

If you share it, each question also sends: who the profile is (profile name or relationship), age, blood type, allergies, conditions, medicines and doses, surgeries, notes, family doctor (name and phone), preferred hospital, and the relationship of your emergency contacts. Never your own name, nor your contacts' names or numbers. This data is used only to answer you.

If you enter a relative's data, make sure they agree, or that you are acting in their interest in an emergency.

Versions before 4.6: the assistant sends the chosen profile's record with each question without asking, including the full name, insurance details and the names, numbers and e-mail addresses of the emergency contacts, and keeps conversations on the phone without this encryption. Update RESQ+ to choose what is shared.

5. Legal bases

We process personal data: to provide the service you ask for (contract); with your explicit consent to send health data to the AI; to protect life or health when you trigger an SOS (vital interest); for our legitimate interests in security, preventing abuse and improving the app; and when the law requires it (for example, accounting records).

6. Where your data goes

Our cloud database and files (Google Firebase) are located in the United States. The First Aid Assistant and automatic translation use Google Cloud in Singapore and the United States. Google Maps Platform, Resend (which keeps a history of the e-mails it sends) and RevenueCat operate in the United States; Open-Meteo has servers in Europe and North America and keeps its server logs, which can contain the position, for 90 days. These providers process data on our behalf, under their data-processing terms, and only for the purposes above. We do not sell your data and we do not use it for advertising.

7. How long we keep it

Automated jobs apply these limits every day:

  • Profile and account: as long as your account exists; deleted with your profile or your account.
  • Photos, voice notes and videos of community or circle alerts: 7 days after upload.
  • Community alerts resolved or cancelled, with their messages and reports: deleted once 7 days have passed since they were posted.
  • Community alerts hidden after reports, or left active: deleted once 30 days have passed since they were posted (time to contest a hiding).
  • Circle alerts and messages: as long as the circle exists; everything under a circle is deleted with it.
  • SOS: the recordings, the position history, the list of contacts alerted and the live-tracking link are deleted 30 days after the SOS; until then they stay available to you, your authorised contacts and, if needed, the authorities.
  • Trips, trip notifications and meeting points: 30 days.
  • Reports about a person, the review queue and weekly statistical summaries: 12 months, even if the account concerned is deleted, to prevent abuse.
  • First Aid Assistant: conversations stay on your phone (last 100 messages); the daily message count is kept with your account.
  • Database backups: a rolling 7-day window, used only to recover from an incident.
  • Subscription records: while the subscription is active, then as long as accounting or tax law requires.

8. Your rights

Under Thailand's Personal Data Protection Act, you can access your data and receive a copy, have it corrected, have it deleted, object to or restrict its use, receive it in a portable format, withdraw a consent at any time (without affecting what was done before), and complain to the Office of the Personal Data Protection Committee (PDPC).

You can delete your account and its data from the app (Settings) or at resqapps.com/delete-account.html. For any other request, write to sales@sornsawan.com; we answer within 30 days.

9. Security

Data is encrypted in transit and at rest. Access to the database is limited by security rules and by Firebase App Check, which only accepts the genuine app. No system is perfectly secure; if a breach affected your data, we would inform you and the authorities as the law requires.

10. Children

RESQ+ is not intended for children under 13. A parent or guardian can create a profile for their child and remains responsible for it. If you think we hold data about a child without such consent, contact us and we will delete it.

11. Changes and contact

We update this policy when the app changes; the date at the top shows the last revision. If a change affects a consent you gave, we will ask you again in the app. Questions: sales@sornsawan.com.

Google Play — Accessibility Services Disclosure

12. Use of the Android Accessibility API

RESQ+ exposes an optional feature that uses Android's Accessibility API to detect a simultaneous long-press of the Volume + and Volume − hardware buttons as a panic gesture. This document discloses, in full transparency, the precise behaviour and data flow of that feature so that users can give informed consent before enabling it.

12.1 Opt-in only

The Accessibility-based trigger is disabled by default. It is only activated when the user (a) flips a dedicated toggle inside RESQ+ Settings, (b) reads and accepts an in-app "Prominent Disclosure" dialog, and (c) manually enables the RESQ+ Accessibility service in Android System Settings → Accessibility. All three steps are required. The toggle can be reversed at any time from the same Settings screen, which clears the consent flag instantly.

12.2 Purpose — who this feature is for

The feature exists to help users who cannot reach or operate the in-app SOS button reliably, including:

  • users with motor or cognitive impairment who can press hardware keys but cannot navigate touch-screen UIs under stress;
  • users physically restrained by an aggressor and unable to look at the screen;
  • users in low-light or zero-visibility environments;
  • users whose phone is in a pocket, bag or holster.

The Accessibility API is the only Android mechanism that allows hardware-key detection while the screen is locked. No alternative API offers this capability for third-party apps.

12.3 What data the service accesses

When enabled, the RESQ+ Accessibility service reads only:

  • The KEY_DOWN and KEY_UP events of KEYCODE_VOLUME_UP and KEYCODE_VOLUME_DOWN only;
  • The timestamps of those two key transitions, used solely to measure the 2-second and 5-second hold thresholds.

12.4 What the service NEVER accesses

RESQ+ does not, under any circumstance, access:

  • screen content, window content, view hierarchies or UI dumps;
  • text fields, passwords, form inputs, clipboard or selection events;
  • notifications from other apps or system notifications;
  • the list of installed apps, app usage, or foreground app data;
  • browsing history, search queries or URLs;
  • any AccessibilityEvent other than the two hardware-volume key events;
  • biometric data, fingerprints, face data or authentication state.

The Accessibility service is configured with the narrowest possible event mask (flagRequestFilterKeyEvents only — no typeWindowContentChanged, no typeViewTextChanged, no typeNotificationStateChanged).

12.5 On-device processing

Volume-key detection runs 100% on-device. No keypress, timestamp or accessibility event is ever uploaded to RESQ+ servers, Firebase, analytics or any third party. The 10-second pre-roll buffer used to capture the trigger gesture lives exclusively in RAM and is discarded if no SOS is launched.

Only when an SOS is explicitly triggered by the user (via the 2-second hold, the in-app button, the Quick Settings Tile or the RESQ+ watch app) does RESQ+ start uploading the event log and 4-second video fragments to Firebase, reachable only through the private SOS link sent to the user's emergency contacts, and deleted 30 days after the SOS (section 7).

12.6 No misuse — Play Store policy alignment

RESQ+ confirms that its Accessibility service is used exclusively to assist users with the panic gesture described above. It is never used for: advertising, analytics, tracking, automation of other apps, scraping content, capturing credentials, password autofill, in-app purchase manipulation, or any usage that would violate the Google Play Accessibility API policy.

12.7 How to disable

You may revoke consent at any time using either of the following methods (a single one is sufficient):

  • open RESQ+ → Settings → toggle Volume Buttons SOS Trigger to OFF;
  • open Android System Settings → Accessibility → RESQ+ Volume SOS Trigger → OFF;
  • uninstall RESQ+.

Questions about this disclosure, or requests for technical or legal review of the implementation, can be addressed to sales@sornsawan.com. We commit to responding within 30 days.
